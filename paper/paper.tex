\begin{abstract}
Federated fine-tuning lets multiple hospitals collaboratively adapt a shared medical imaging
backbone without pooling patient data, but cross-specialty heterogeneity -- where each hospital's
data distribution reflects a different medical specialty rather than a random partition of one
distribution -- breaks the implicit i.i.d.\ assumption behind naive aggregation schemes like
FedAvg. We study this setting with a fully simulated, CPU-compatible pipeline across
\textbf{two} independent specialty mixes drawn from MedMNIST: Dataset A (pathology, dermatology,
blood, retina, kidney tissue) and Dataset B (abdominal organ, chest pneumonia, retina fundus,
breast ultrasound, abdominal organ-C), each mapped to five simulated hospitals. Each client
fine-tunes a frozen pretrained image backbone via LoRA adapters, communicating only the low-rank
adapter factors. We propose \textbf{FedSAA} (Similarity-Attended Aggregation), which forms each
client's merged LoRA delta $U_i = B_iA_i$, computes a softmax-attention aggregate over other
clients' deltas weighted by cosine similarity, blends it with the uniform mean via a $\lambda$
parameter, re-factorizes the result to a personalized rank-$r$ adapter per client via truncated
SVD, and then blends that server-computed adapter back toward the client's own pre-aggregation
adapter via a retention coefficient $\alpha$, followed by a brief additional local fine-tuning
pass. FedSAA reduces exactly to FedAvg-on-merged-deltas at $\lambda=1$ (proved in
\texttt{tests/test\_fedsaa.py}).
\todoresults{global accuracy, macro-F1, AUC, worst-client accuracy, and communication cost for
FedSAA vs.\ Centralized/Local-only/FedAvg/FedProx on both datasets, with statistical significance
across 5 seeds, once configs/full\_run.yaml has been run to completion.}
\todoresults{ablation findings for $\lambda$, $\tau$, rank $r$, and retention $\alpha$.}
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Federated Learning}
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} established sample-weighted parameter averaging as the default
aggregation rule; FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} adds a proximal term to stabilize local updates
under statistical heterogeneity. Both are implemented here as baselines
(\texttt{src/federated/loop.py}, \texttt{client.py}) for direct, same-codebase comparison.

\subsection{LoRA / Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)}
LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} freezes a pretrained backbone and injects trainable low-rank factors
$(A,B)$ into target layers, drastically reducing trainable/communicated parameters relative to
full fine-tuning. Naively averaging LoRA factors $(A_i,B_i)$ directly is a biased approximation
of averaging the effective weight update; FedSAA's $\lambda=1$ case is explicitly defined at the
level of merged deltas $U_i=B_iA_i$ to sidestep this (Section~\ref{sec:method-fedavg-equiv}).

\section{Method: FedSAA (Similarity-Attended Aggregation)}
\label{sec:method}

Let client $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ hold LoRA factors $(A_i, B_i)$ for a given target layer after
local training. Per round, per layer, the server
(\texttt{src/federated/fedsaa.py::fedsaa\_aggregate}) computes:

\begin{align}
  U_i &= B_i A_i \quad \text{(flattened)} \label{eq:merge} \\
  S_{ij} &= \cos(U_i, U_j) \label{eq:sim} \\
  w_{ij} &= \mathrm{softmax}_j\!\left(S_{ij} / \tau\right) \label{eq:attn} \\
  \bar{U}_i &= \sum_j w_{ij} U_j, \qquad G = \frac{1}{n}\sum_j U_j \label{eq:agg} \\
  \tilde{U}_i &= (1-\lambda)\,\bar{U}_i + \lambda\, G \label{eq:blend} \\
  (A_i, B_i)_{\text{global}} &= \mathrm{TruncatedSVD}_r(\tilde{U}_i) \label{eq:svd}
\end{align}

where step~\eqref{eq:svd} re-factorizes $\tilde{U}_i$ via truncated rank-$r$ SVD:
$B_{\text{global}} = U_{\text{svd}}[:,{:}r]\sqrt{S_{\text{svd}}[{:}r]}$,
$A_{\text{global}} = \sqrt{S_{\text{svd}}[{:}r]}\,V_{\text{svd}}^\top[{:}r,:]$, reshaped back to
conv-kernel shape. Unlike FedAvg, the result at this point is already \textbf{personalized}:
every client would get back its own $(A_i,B_i)$, not one broadcast global adapter.

\subsection{Client-local training}
Each client trains its LoRA adapter + local classifier head for \texttt{local\_epochs} epochs of
SGD (\texttt{src/federated/client.py::local\_train}); the head is never aggregated or
communicated, since specialties have different numbers of classes.

\subsection{Communication accounting}
Only the LoRA $(A,B)$ factors are exchanged
(\texttt{get\_adapter\_state}/\texttt{set\_adapter\_state}), independent of the frozen backbone's
parameters, which never move.

\subsection{FedAvg as a special case}
\label{sec:method-fedavg-equiv}
At $\lambda=1$, $\tilde{U}_i = G$ for \emph{every} client, independent of $\tau$ or $w_{ij}$ --
i.e.\ steps~\eqref{eq:merge}--\eqref{eq:svd} collapse to the rank-$r$ truncated SVD of the plain
mean of merged deltas $\frac{1}{n}\sum_j B_jA_j$: exactly ``FedAvg on merged LoRA deltas.''
Verified to floating-point precision across multiple $\tau$ values in
\texttt{tests/test\_fedsaa.py::test\_lambda1\_matches\_fedavg\_on\_merged\_deltas}. At
$\lambda=0$, clients receive purely self-attended, personalized aggregates that are (by
construction) generally distinct across clients.

\subsection{Local adapter retention}
\label{sec:method-retention}
The server-computed personalized adapter $(A_i,B_i)_{\text{global}}$ from
step~\eqref{eq:svd} is then blended with client $i$'s \textbf{own pre-aggregation local adapter}
-- the one it sent into this round's aggregation, before
steps~\eqref{eq:merge}--\eqref{eq:svd} touched it, denoted $(A_i,B_i)_{\text{local}}$ -- via a
retention coefficient $\alpha \in [0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  (A_i, B_i)_{\text{final}} = (1-\alpha)\,(A_i,B_i)_{\text{global}} + \alpha\,(A_i,B_i)_{\text{local}}
  \label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
applied per LoRA tensor ($A$ and $B$ separately, same shapes, elementwise). $\alpha=0$ is pure
FedSAA (Sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:method-fedavg-equiv}, no retention); $\alpha \to 1$
increasingly ignores the server's aggregation and keeps the client's own just-trained adapter.
Implemented in \texttt{src/federated/fedsaa.py::fedsaa\_aggregate} (the \texttt{alpha} parameter).

\subsection{Post-aggregation local adaptation}
\label{sec:method-postadapt}
After retention-blending, each participating client runs a brief additional local fine-tuning
pass -- \texttt{post\_agg\_adapt\_steps} SGD mini-batches (not a full epoch) on its own data
(\texttt{src/federated/client.py::local\_adapt\_steps}) -- before evaluation. Without this step,
``personalization'' would only ever happen at the server; the client's adapter would never
actually see its own data again after being overwritten by the retention-blended result.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Source of truth for every value below: \texttt{configs/full\_run.yaml}. Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters}

\subsection{Datasets (two independent cross-specialty non-IID partitions)}
All experiments are simulation-based; \textbf{no real patient data is used anywhere in this
pipeline.} Two independently selectable specialty mixes are drawn from MedMNIST, each mapping
five specialty subsets to five simulated hospitals (\texttt{data.dataset\_presets}):

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Client & Dataset A & Dataset B \\
\midrule
0 & PathMNIST (pathology, 9 cls.) & OrganAMNIST (abdominal organ, 11 cls.) \\
1 & DermaMNIST (dermatology, 7 cls.) & PneumoniaMNIST (chest pneumonia, 2 cls.) \\
2 & BloodMNIST (blood, 8 cls.) & RetinaMNIST (retina fundus, 5 cls.) \\
3 & OCTMNIST (retina OCT, 4 cls.) & BreastMNIST (breast ultrasound, 2 cls.) \\
4 & TissueMNIST (kidney tissue, 8 cls.) & OrganCMNIST (abdominal organ, 11 cls.) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Both mixes draw exclusively from the MedMNIST v2 suite (no new external data source). Images are
converted grayscale$\to$3-channel RGB and loaded at $28\times28$; no per-client subsample cap is
applied for the full run, unlike the \texttt{configs/local\_debug.yaml} smoke-test profile.

\subsection{Backbone and adapters}
Frozen pretrained \textbf{ResNet-18}; LoRA attached to \texttt{conv1} and every Conv2d inside
\texttt{layer1}--\texttt{layer4}, rank $r{=}8$, $\alpha_{\text{LoRA}}{=}8$, dropout$=0.05$ (this
$\alpha_{\text{LoRA}}$ is the standard LoRA scaling factor, unrelated to FedSAA's retention
$\alpha$, Section~\ref{sec:method-retention}). Each client keeps its own classifier head, never
aggregated.

\subsection{Federated protocol}
5 clients, 60 rounds, 2 local epochs, client fraction $C{=}1.0$ (full participation); local SGD,
lr$=0.01$, momentum$=0.9$, weight decay$=10^{-4}$.

\subsection{FedSAA hyperparameters}
$\tau=0.5$, $\lambda=0.5$, \texttt{svd\_rank}$=8$; local adapter retention $\alpha=0.3$
(Section~\ref{sec:method-retention}); post-aggregation local adaptation
\texttt{post\_agg\_adapt\_steps}$=5$ (Section~\ref{sec:method-postadapt}). FedProx baseline:
$\mu=0.01$.

\subsection{Seeds and reproducibility}
5 seeds ($\{0,1,2,3,4\}$) for every method on every dataset, deterministic seeding.

\subsection{FedSAA ablation grid}
One-at-a-time sweeps around the defaults above:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Axis & Values & Seeds/config \\
\midrule
$\lambda$ & $\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$ & 1 \\
$\tau$ & $\{0.1, 0.5, 1\}$ & 1 \\
rank $r$ & $\{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ & 1 \\
retention $\alpha$ & $\{0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5\}$ & 3 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The retention axis runs more seeds than the others because it directly affects the paper's
personalization claim (Sections~\ref{sec:method-retention}--\ref{sec:method-postadapt}). The
grid is one-at-a-time, not a full product, to keep the sweep tractable on commodity hardware.

\subsection{Baselines}
Centralized (upper bound: one shared LoRA adapter trained jointly on pooled data), Local-only
(lower bound: no communication ever), FedAvg and FedProx, all logging the identical
\texttt{metrics.csv} schema as FedSAA for direct comparison, on both Dataset A and Dataset B.

\subsection{Hardware and environment}
Developed and smoke-tested on a CPU-only machine (no CUDA GPU); \texttt{configs/full\_run.yaml}'s
\texttt{device: cpu} reflects that -- flip to \texttt{cuda} locally to run on a GPU machine.
